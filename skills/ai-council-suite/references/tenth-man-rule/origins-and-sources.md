# Origins and sources

## The failure

Before 6 October 1973 Israeli military intelligence worked inside *HaKonseptzia* — "the Concept": Egypt would not go to war before it could neutralize Israeli air superiority, and Syria would not attack without Egypt. Under that frame, Egyptian and Syrian mobilization was read as exercise, signalling, or insufficient evidence. The attack across the Suez Canal and onto the Golan achieved tactical surprise.

The indicators were in hand: forward deployments, canal-crossing preparations, unusual coordination and timing. They were interpreted, not missed. A southern-command intelligence officer, Lieutenant Binyamin Siman-Tov, read them as preparation for war and could not get his assessment elevated past intermediate commanders.

The Agranat Commission (established November 1973; interim findings April 1974; final report January 1975) located the failure in assessment, warning and communication — not in collection.

## The institutional response

- **Ipcha Mistabra** (Aramaic: "the opposite is proven") — a dedicated challenge function in AMAN, the Military Intelligence Directorate.
- **The Review Department** — assigned to examine whether the dominant assessment might be wrong: identify unverified assumptions, build alternative explanations, challenge readings of adversary intent, examine low-probability strategic surprises, and carry alternative assessments to senior leadership. In 1983 its head was moved directly under the Director of AMAN rather than staying inside the research hierarchy.
- **The Siman-Tov procedure** — protection for minority assessments so an intermediate commander cannot block them.

## What is Hollywood

The literal rule — "if nine agree, the tenth must disagree" — was popularized by the 2013 film *World War Z*. It is not an IDF regulation. The real practice was a professional institutional challenge function, not a numerical voting procedure.

> The objective is not to assume that the majority is wrong. It is to prevent the majority's confidence from becoming a substitute for independently tested evidence.

## What the CIA took from the same failure

American intelligence held comparable indications and reached comparable conclusions, judging the probability of war by whether Egypt and Syria *could win* rather than whether they had political reasons to fight. A limited war that shifted the diplomatic stalemate counted as a win for Sadat, and that possibility sat in a May 1973 National Intelligence Estimate without being carried into the assessments immediately before 6 October.

- **27 October 1973** — DCI William Colby's memorandum *Critique of Middle East Crisis* argued the deficiency was interpretation, not information, and proposed systematic challenge of dominant assessments, protection of minority interpretations, designated devil's advocates, and adversarial procedures.
- **1974–75** — the Product Review Division drafted a formal challenge mechanism. It failed to become a permanent community-wide institution; critics argued manufactured disagreement would confuse policymakers and divert resources.
- **1976** — the Team A/Team B experiment showed both the value and the distortion risk of competitive analysis: participants rewarded for defending a predetermined conclusion stop evaluating evidence.
- **Post-9/11** — the CIA Red Cell; DIA devil's-advocate functions.
- **2004** — the Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act assigned the DNI responsibility for sound tradecraft and red-team analysis.
- **ICD 203** *(Analytic Standards)* — separate information, assumptions and judgment; characterize uncertainty and confidence accurately; consider alternatives; identify information gaps and the developments that would change the judgment.
- **2009** — *A Tradecraft Primer* codified structured techniques: analysis of competing hypotheses, key-assumptions check, devil's advocacy, red team, indicators-based warning.

## The eight lessons, stated for general use

1. Collection is not understanding. More information under a defective frame produces no better warning.
2. Judge the other side's intent independently of your own success criteria. An actor can rationally start a fight they expect to lose militarily, because their win is political.
3. Consensus is a conclusion to be tested, not an authority that establishes truth. Shared training, sources and incentives produce agreement without independent confirmation.
4. Warning fatigue is not disconfirmation. Earlier alerts that came to nothing do not prove the next one false; ask what distinguishes this pattern.
5. Probability is not consequence. Low-probability, high-impact, short-warning-time scenarios earn sustained examination regardless of the odds.
6. Dissent must reach the decider. An assessment that is produced and then smoothed away by the hierarchy has failed its purpose.
7. Monitoring is not warning. Documenting activity is not the same as stating that the risk has risen.
8. Assessments are provisional. Date every judgment and revalidate it when the environment moves.

## The counter-example: 7 October 2023

Fifty years and one day after the Yom Kippur attack, the mechanism existed and did not save the decision. Investigators pointed to the deterrence assumption, to the *Jericho Wall* plan being known without being believed, and to a gap between recognizing a threat and changing readiness. The institutional lesson is the one this skill repeats: a challenge function that cannot alter assumptions, warning judgments or readiness is not a safeguard — it is a ritual. Possessing a plan is not the same as accepting that someone will execute it.
