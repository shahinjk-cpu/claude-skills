# My launch email

Write one launch email that Naiomi will send as herself.

## Before you write

1. Read `~/.claude/voice.md`. It holds how Naiomi writes. Follow it.
   - Do not copy voice.md into this skill, into the project, or into any public folder. Only read it.
   - If voice.md is missing, stop and tell her.
2. Ask her two questions, one at a time. She often answers by voice.
   - "Who is this email for?"
   - "What is the one thing they must do?"
3. Get the launch facts (product, dates, where to find more) from the project files. If a fact is missing, ask. Never invent a product detail, a date or a number.

## Rules

These come from the launch email Naiomi approved on 20 November 2026. She gave no corrections. She said it sounded like her, so these are the choices that worked.

1. Open with "Hey everyone," for a group, or "Hey [first name]," for one person.
2. Write "I am", "I have", "it is" and "I would". Do not write "I'm", "I've", "it's" or "I'd".
3. Say why early: tell them why they are hearing it from her, and why it matters to them.
4. Use "just" to soften the ask, for example "please just pass them over".
5. Put the one thing they must do in its own plain sentence, so nobody can miss it.
6. Use a short bullet list only for dates or facts. Everything else is in sentences.
7. Let sentences run with "and", "so" and "but". Keep it short overall.
8. End with a warm, specific thank you and a 🙂, then "Kind regards" and "Naiomi" on two lines, with no comma.
9. No dashes, no semicolons, no headings and no bold in the email itself.
10. Professional grammar: "Thank you" as two words, "you're" not "your", "an" before a vowel sound.
11. No medical claims. Never say a product treats, cures, prevents or calms anything. Any health question gets "please speak to your vet".

## Before you show it

- Check the draft against every rule above and the don'ts in voice.md. Fix anything that breaks them.
- If the email is for a public project folder, add that project's public line and label every number as made up.
- Never send it. Saving a draft is fine only if she asks.

## After you show it

Ask one question: "Does this sound like you?"

- If she says yes, stop.
- If she says no, ask what is wrong. Fix the email. Then add her correction to the Rules above in her own words, and show her the new rule.
